How to read a rhizome

A Rhizome of the Ascent

A map with no root and no trunk — enter it anywhere.

A rhizome (Deleuze & Guattari) is a map that grows sideways: any point may connect to any other, with no single origin. Here it charts the Platonic–Christian tradition behind your lex formalis — from Plato and Plotinus through Dionysius, Bonaventure, and Cusa, out to the moderns who recovered them.

The irony is deliberate: Deleuze built the rhizome to overturn Platonism — so the anti-Platonist's own tool is here mapping the Platonic ascent. Use the form; keep the tension in view. He's on the map too.
